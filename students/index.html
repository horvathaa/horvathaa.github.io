Prospective students

I’d love to hear from students who want to study how people build, understand, and change software. I’m an assistant professor of Software Engineering in the Department of Computer Science at the University of Victoria and a co-PI of the CHISEL Lab.

What we might work on

Imagine inheriting a feature that someone built with an AI agent. The tests pass, but you need to change how it behaves. What would you want to know first? Where would you look, and how would you tell whether an explanation was trustworthy? These are questions we can investigate by talking with developers, observing them at work, or building a tool and studying how people use it. CLAI is one example.

What I’m looking for

I’m interested in students who are curious about developers’ experiences and willing to revise an idea after seeing how someone actually works. You might enjoy interviewing people, building prototypes, or tracing a puzzling decision through a codebase. Tell me which part appeals to you or which you’d like to learn.

How to get in touch

Explore one of my projects or demos, or a paper linked from my research page. Choose something that makes you curious. You’re welcome to start a conversation while you’re still working through an idea.

I’d like to hear how you understand the work, in your own words. Tell me what led you to an interpretation, where you got stuck, or what you might question. It’s absolutely okay to be unsure or to disagree with something I’ve written. There’s room to work through misunderstandings together.

Email amberhorvath9@gmail.com with the subject “Prospective student – [research topic] – [start term].” In roughly 200–300 words altogether, please share:

  • What caught your attention? Point to a specific detail in the work and explain what you found interesting, surprising, confusing, or worth questioning.
  • How does it connect to you? Describe an experience, observation, or problem that helps explain your interest. This could come from coursework, employment, a personal project, or everyday life. Prior research experience is welcome, and so are people getting started.
  • What would you like to investigate? Share a question you’d enjoy exploring and why it matters to you. An unfinished idea is welcome.

Please also include the opportunity you’re seeking, your intended start date, and your CV. Use whatever writing format feels natural to you. A short email is enough.

Using AI

You’re welcome to use AI to explore the work, translate, or improve your writing. Please make sure the experiences and interests you describe are yours, and that you could discuss the ideas in your email—including what you’re still unsure about. I value hearing your perspective, however you express it.

About replies

I receive more inquiries than I can answer individually. I prioritize messages that explain a specific research connection and match my current supervision capacity. You may send one follow-up after two weeks. You can submit an application to UVic even if you haven’t received a reply from me.

Applying to UVic

For graduate study, submit your formal application through UVic. The Computer Science MSc and Computer Science PhD pages list requirements, deadlines, and how to name a prospective supervisor.
